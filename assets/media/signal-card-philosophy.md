# Evidentiary Clarity

**Philosophy:** Truth made legible. Every claim carries its receipt. The surface
reads in a glance; the rigor waits one layer beneath, available to anyone who
looks closer. This is the aesthetic of the honest analyst — confident enough to
show the counterargument, disciplined enough to never raise its voice.

**Space and form.** The composition is a single vertical column of evidence,
descending like a forensic report. Generous negative space carries authority;
nothing is crowded, because crowding signals panic and this artifact is calm.
Hairline rules and numbered section markers (01–08) impose the systematic order
of a laboratory notebook — the sense that each assertion was logged, indexed, and
checked. The eye moves top to bottom with the inevitability of a proof.

**Color and material.** Near-black ground, the color of a terminal at rest, faintly
warmed by green so it never reads as pure void. A single electric lime is the
instrument's only loud voice — rationed with severity, reserved for the live
pulse, the confidence fill, the connective arrows of the causal chain. Amber
appears exactly once, marking the counterargument, because honesty deserves its
own hue. Everything else is warm bone-white and muted graphite. The restraint is
the point: color is information, never decoration.

**Scale and rhythm.** Typographic hierarchy does the explaining. A serif headline
carries the human meaning with editorial weight; monospace labels and receipts
carry the machine precision underneath. The cadence alternates — assertion, then
evidence; claim, then caveat — a steady systolic rhythm that mirrors how a
careful mind actually reasons. Small clinical labels in tracked capitals behave
like reference markers in a scientific plate.

**Composition and balance.** The grid is invisible but absolute. Margins are
sacred; no element touches an edge, nothing overlaps, every baseline is
deliberate. The faint background grid and a single restrained glow at the crown
suggest an instrument's screen without imitating one. The whole must look
meticulously crafted — the product of countless hours, painstaking alignment, and
master-level typographic judgment, as though set by someone at the very top of
their field who chose to remove everything that wasn't necessary.

**Visual hierarchy.** What happened, who is exposed, why it matters — then the
chain, the receipts, the confidence, the doubt, the watch list. Information lives
in structure, not adjectives. The result should feel less like marketing and more
like an artifact: something a serious reader screenshots not because it is loud,
but because it is unmistakably the product of expertise and care.
